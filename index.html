OPENING NOTE

Running gear isn't fashion first. It's a set of small decisions — fabric, fit, seam placement — that either disappear on the run or remind you they exist at kilometre six.

This guide exists to explain those decisions plainly, with no product to sell you at the end of it.

01 — THE BASICS

Anatomy of a running kit

A full kit is really four separate systems working together. Understanding what each one is for makes it much easier to judge whether a piece of clothing will help you or fight you.

01

Base layer

Sits against skin. Its only job is moving sweat off the body and into the air, so it should feel almost dry even mid-run.

02

Mid layer

Traps a thin layer of warm air in cooler weather. Needed on cold mornings, unnecessary — even harmful — in heat.

03

Outer shell

Blocks wind and rain. The best shells breathe almost as well as they repel, so effort doesn't turn into a sauna.

04

Footwear

Absorbs impact and returns energy on every stride — the single piece of kit that changes how a run actually feels.

02 — MATERIALS & FIT

Fabric and fit, in plain terms

Marketing language around running fabrics can get noisy. Here's what's actually happening in the weave, stitched together with a few real details worth checking before you buy anything.

FIG. 01

Moisture-wicking weaves

Fabric that pulls, not soaks

Technical fabrics use fine synthetic fibres woven so sweat spreads across a wide surface and evaporates fast, instead of pooling in cotton's tight, absorbent weave. That's the whole trick behind "wicking" — it's a shape of the fabric, not a coating sprayed on top.

  • Best for long runs, humid weather
  • Watch for seams that sit flat against skin
FIG. 02

Fit and freedom of movement

Compression vs. relaxed cut

Compression kit applies gentle, even pressure to support muscles on longer efforts, while relaxed cuts prioritise airflow and unrestricted stride. Neither is "correct" — the right choice depends on distance, temperature, and honestly, personal comfort.

  • Compression suits tempo runs, recovery days
  • Relaxed suits easy runs, hot climates
FIG. 03

Visibility & low-light detailing

Reflective elements, done well

Good reflective detailing sits at the ankles and wrists, where the movement of running catches headlights at the widest angle — not just as a printed logo on the chest, which barely helps a driver notice you at all.

  • Essential for early mornings, monsoon evenings
  • Look for reflective strips on moving joints

03 — WEATHER

Dressing for the season you're actually running in

The same kit rarely works across a full year. Here's a rough starting point for three very different conditions — treat it as a baseline to adjust, not a rulebook.

32°+

Summer heat

Lightweight, loose-fitting, light-coloured fabric that reflects heat rather than trapping it. Ventilation panels at the back and underarms matter more than anything else here.

SingletsMesh panelsLight colours
MON

Monsoon runs

Water-resistant (not fully waterproof — that traps heat) outer layers, quick-drying fabric underneath, and grippier outsoles for wet pavement and trail mud alike.

Cold mornings

Thin layering beats one thick layer — trapped air between layers insulates better, and you can shed a layer once your body warms up mid-run.

04 — DECISION GUIDE

How to choose your kit, step by step

A short, practical sequence for narrowing down what to actually put on before a run — useful whether you're kitting out for the first time or rebuilding after a few seasons.

01

Start with the run, not the wardrobe

Distance and pace change what you need. A 5K tempo run and a three-hour long run put very different demands on fabric and support.

02

Check the forecast, not the calendar

Season is a rough guide; the actual temperature, humidity, and wind on the day should decide your layers.

03

Fit before features

A perfectly wicking fabric still chafes if it's cut wrong. Try kit in the same motion you'll actually run in, not standing still.

04

Add visibility if you run in low light

Early mornings and evenings call for reflective detailing regardless of how warm or cool the weather is.

05

Test on a short run before a long one

New kit deserves a trial 5K before it's trusted on race day or a long run — small irritations only show up once you're moving.

06 — LONGEVITY

Getting more runs out of the kit you already own

Technical fabric loses its wicking ability long before it looks worn out. A few habits keep it working the way it was designed to.

01
Skip the fabric softener

It coats synthetic fibres and blocks the exact wicking action you paid for.

02
Wash in cold water

Heat breaks down elastane fibres faster, loosening compression fit over time.

03
Turn kit inside out

Protects printed logos and reflective detailing from direct friction in the wash.

04
Air-dry, out of direct sun

Tumble drying and harsh sunlight both degrade elastic fibres and reflective coatings.

05
Rotate your shoes

Alternating between two pairs lets cushioning foam fully recover between runs.

06
Retire kit by feel, not age

Once fabric stays damp longer or elastic feels loose, it's past doing its job well.
